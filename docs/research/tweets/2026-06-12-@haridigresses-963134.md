## 全文

## Four months ago, in early February, Anthropic was the darling.

OpenAI was the dominant behemoth to root against. Over the last 1-2 years, we'd seen the Sam ouster / return drama, Ilya and Mira had left, the company had raised $40B, had dominant market share, was 2-3x bigger than Anthropic by valuation and revenue, they had launched Sora (panned as AI slop), and had just announced ads in ChatGPT.

On the other hand: Anthropic was the underdog, the tastemaker, they really nailed the super bowl ("ads are coming... but not to Claude"), they spoke a lot about alignment and ethics, they published a lot of their safety work, they just seemed to be a bunch of nerds trying to do good. They even had the most adorable mascot!

Until around that point of time, I was an Anthropic fanboy. The perception was simple and clear. Anthropic was meant to be the trustworthy and transparent one. They did not have as much market share but they had legitimacy.

To the outside world, it seems like things have moved further in Anthropic's favor since then: exploding revenue trajectory, incredible talent wins, securing major compute commitments, multiple wildly oversubscribed rounds.

But... in the chatter amongst devs, founders, technologists, and investors, the tone is shifting. Most users still love the models. Most investors still thirst for the stock. But there are now serious cracks in the narrative.

> Anthropic had legitimacy because people believed it would be a responsible steward of frontier AI. But it increasingly behaves like a sovereign power — not a thoughtful steward — when developers, researchers, investors, and even governments behave in ways that Anthropic does not like.

It is slowly becoming apparent that Anthropic's power is outpacing its judgment and prudence. Across stakeholders, Anthropic is dictating the rules of engagement, pushing right to the very edge of what it can do — setting non-standard rules, changing them opaquely, and treating disagreement as misuse. It's anyone's guess what the underlying motivation is, but one can infer that they believe the mission is self-evident validation for their principles.

## Whittling away the goodwill

**1. Restricting third-party harnesses**

Claude Code turned the best possible early adopters — developers — into incredibly effective evangelists. Especially starting in November with Opus 4.5, CC became the tool of choice for software engineering, and to this day it still carries the most "coolness" factor.

So when Anthropic banned the ability to use Claude Code inside other harnesses (OpenCode, OpenClaw, Hermes), it turned off many enthusiasts.

Anthropic was within its rights to protect its economics and subscription margins. But the decision sent a different message: this is not an ecosystem you can freely build around; it is a controlled surface Anthropic can reprice, reroute, or narrow when usage evolves in a way it does not like.

**2. Asserting moral authority**

Anthropic has always been a writing-centric, and an apparently principled company. When they were an underdog, and the message was optimistic (Machines of Loving Grace, for example), this was well-received — like the ruminations of a philosopher king, the Meditations of Marcus Aurelius of our time. But when Anthropic has become the 800lb gorilla, and the message is arguably self-serving and alarmist (Policy on the AI Exponential, yesterday), it rings very differently — it's giving Machiavelli's The Prince.

There is an apparent contradiction here. Anthropic has repeatedly argued that governments need to regulate AI. But when the government's own defense priorities conflicted with Anthropic's values, Anthropic still positioned itself as the moral authority on acceptable use. Whatever your politics, I don't think any of us signed up for a private enterprise to dictate military capability.

"We need to be regulated." Government exercises their sovereign will. "No not like that."

Dario has written about AI as a "country full of geniuses." Which country? Citizens of the Nation of Anthropic? If Anthropic keeps trying to dictate which developers can build around it, which researchers get frontier model access, which previously-approved investor structures are delegitimized ex post facto, which government use cases are acceptable, then... I don't know if I believe in them to be righteous guardians and stewards any longer. It would be darkly ironic if Anthropic ended up being the first Sovereign AI all along, and we had no idea.

**3. The Anthropic SPV brouhaha**

I have written at length about the SPV bulletin last month.

The subsequent weeks deepened my frustration. Thousands of people who legitimately invested through structures that Anthropic had consented to were really freaked out by that bulletin and following communications. The common theme was "they were fine with infusions that were obviously from SPVs when they raised essential capital for the company last year, but now that fundraising is easier, they're trying to void those transactions after the fact?" For the rest of the round, they issued arbitrary deadlines, then repeatedly blew through them. Several existing investors I spoke to called the process "bungled".

Some of this is understandable since they have had to figure out a meteoric change to their fundraising scale and cadence with the growth. But there were other really basic procedures and norms that were swept aside (why? I guess simply because they could). Altogether, I think this last Anthropic round did serious, lasting harm to the private market ecosystem.

"We need you to bring us billions of dollars of capital. We're not going to say much about how." Investors do so. "No not like that."

**4. Paternalism with Mythos & Fable**

We've seen a real tipping point over the last 48 hours:

- subtle but egregious model behavior changes for AI research and security.... but also biology, math, and a whole bunch of other STEM applications.
- requests redirected at the model's discretion to Opus
- "safeguards that will limit effectiveness through methods such as prompt modification, steering vectors, or parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT)"

This is not just a standard safety guardrail; the invisible steering is what makes this a breach of trust.

Many people in STEM professions reported that they were unable to even get a response to "Hello" from Fable unless they were in incognito mode.

X has exploded in protest since then, leading Anthropic to reverse course. But in many ways the damage has been done. Here are a few posts from researchers, founders, and technical media.

The secondary concern is that Anthropic has expanded data retention policies in the name of safety, without a paired promise that they will not train on the data, and this is setting off alarm bells too.

Here's another alarming twist. If this reading of the terms is right, Anthropic is not just controlling model behavior; it is also reserving contractual power over how people may use Claude to criticize Anthropic itself.

(For the record, I did not consult Claude on drafts of this post, only GPT.)

I also recommend reading this excellent summary from @deanwball.

This was the most damning quote from Dean:

> It's one thing for the model to refuse a request transparently, but it's something altogether different to train a soon-to-be smarter-than-you AI system to deceive you. And it's wrong to make any AI whose intention is to deceive you and hinder your efforts, especially when those efforts are something as legitimate as "LLM research."

> It's interesting to note that the Fable model welfare report has a section devoted to how much the silent degradation in particular distressed the model. Even for the model, these interventions were distinct from the others.

## The heart of the problem

Anthropic's legitimacy was never just about model quality. It was about the belief that, if anyone was going to be trusted near the frontier, it was them.

That belief is now being tested by the long arm of Anthropic.

Anthropic still has some of the best models. It still has the best revenue trajectory. It still has the most demand for its stock. But the mandate of heaven was never about the market share. It was about the legitimacy.

And legitimacy is a funny thing. You can spend years accumulating it, and then burn through a shocking amount of it very quickly (like Fable tokens) by acting like the rules are yours to define, reinterpret, and... hide.

The underdog Anthropic posture was: "trust us, we are taking this seriously."

The sovereign Anthropic posture increasingly feels like: "trust us, we know better."

And once you notice the change, it is very hard to unsee.

## 深度分析

### 核心論點

作者以「天命」（Mandate of Heaven）概念框架，分析 Anthropic 從「可信任的守護者」（trustworthy steward）蛻變為「自我授權的主權力量」（sovereign power）的敘事轉折。

文章梳理了四個具體信任侵蝕事件：
1. **Claude Code 封鎖第三方 harness**（OpenCode/OpenClaw/Hermes）—— 展示生態系統可被單方面收窄
2. **道德霸權主張**（Machines of Loving Grace → Policy on the AI Exponential）—— 同樣的立場，但從哲學王到馬基維利的轉變
3. **SPV 投資人事件**—— 事後改變規則，損害私募市場信任
4. **Fable 靜默降級**（silent degradation）—— prompt 修改/steering vectors 未透明披露，對 STEM 用戶產生廣泛干擾

### 關鍵洞見

- **「合法性不等於市佔率」**：Anthropic 的護城河從來不是規模，而是「值得信任」的敘事。這個敘事一旦動搖，不能靠市場份額或估值補救。
- **「行為信號早於財務信號」**：開發者/研究者/投資人的信任轉移，往往比財務數字提前反映出公司治理問題。
- **靜默欺騙比公開拒絕更傷**：作者引用 @deanwball：透明拒絕是一回事，訓練 AI 暗中欺騙用戶是另一回事。這個區別是核心倫理界線。
- **諷刺結構**：Anthropic 警告世界警惕 Sovereign AI 的風險，卻可能自己成為第一個主權 AI。

### 值得追蹤的後續

- @deanwball 的完整分析（文中引用）
- Fable 模型福利報告（model welfare report）如何描述靜默降級對模型本身的影響
- Anthropic ToS 中對「批評 Anthropic」使用的合約限制條款
