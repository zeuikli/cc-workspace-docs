## 全文

On roughly 1 in 20 prompts, the most capable Claude model ever shipped won't be the one answering you.
Most people will flip the dropdown to Fable 5 today and never notice the three flagged areas where it quietly hands the prompt to a weaker model, or that the free access closes in 13 days.

# What actually shipped

Two models, one brain.
Fable 5 is the new flagship, available to everyone. Mythos 5 is the same underlying model with the cybersecurity safeguards lifted, restricted to Project Glasswing partners and a coming trusted-access program. The only thing separating the two is the safeguards.
The name is the tell: fable comes from the Latin fabula, "that which is told," a sibling in meaning to the Greek mythos. It is the strongest model Anthropic has ever put in front of the public.
On SWE-Bench Pro it scores 80.3% against Opus 4.8's 69.2%, GPT 5.5's 58.6%, and Gemini 3.1 Pro's 54.2%. On Terminal-Bench 2.1, 88.0%. On GDPval-AA knowledge work, 1932 to Opus 4.8's 1890.
The pattern that matters more than any single number: the longer and more complex the task, the bigger the lead. This is not a model that wins on one-liners. It wins on the jobs you've been afraid to hand off.
Price is $10 per million input tokens, $50 per million output. Less than half of what Mythos Preview cost.
The advantage compounds on big work. In early testing, Stripe ran a codebase-wide migration on a 50-million-line Ruby codebase in a single day, work that would have taken a team over two months by hand.

# The silent fallback: ~5% of the time you're not talking to Fable

This is the part nobody is going to read in the announcement, and it changes how you route work.
Fable ships with a set of classifiers: separate systems that watch each prompt. When a request looks like it touches cybersecurity, biology and chemistry, or distillation, the response is handled by Claude Opus 4.8 instead of Fable, and you get told it happened.
More than 95% of sessions never hit a fallback. For those sessions, Fable 5 is effectively identical to Mythos 5. The premium model is the default experience.
But if your work lives in one of those three buckets, you are often getting Opus 4.8, not the flagship you selected. Three things to internalize:

- Security work falls back, by design. The cyber classifier is built to stop Fable from making progress on offensive tasks. Under automated red-teaming, Fable's offensive-task success rate sits at 5.4%, down from Opus 4.8's 56.6% and Opus 4.6's 83.2%. That's not Fable being worse at security. That's the safeguard doing exactly its job. If you do pentest, exploit, or red-team work, expect the fallback and don't waste turns fighting it.
- Bio and chem are netted broadly right now. Anthropic tuned these conservatively to ship safely and fast, which means even benign chemistry or biology questions can trigger a fallback. They've said they'll narrow it. Until then, treat any life-sciences prompt as likely-Opus.
- Distillation-shaped prompts fall back too. Anything that reads like an attempt to extract the model's capabilities to train a competitor gets routed to Opus 4.8.

The tell is simple: you get a notice when it happens. If you see that notice on a topic you didn't expect, that's a false positive, and Anthropic openly admits the net is wide for now. Rephrase, or accept Opus 4.8 for that turn.
Don't read this as a weaker model hidden behind a switch. Read it as routing information: send flagged work knowing Opus answers it, and send everything else to Fable while it's free.

# What they actually upgraded

The bars are loud. Three quieter upgrades matter more for daily work.

- Vision is the real headline.
Fable can rebuild a web app's source code from screenshots alone. The demo that proves it: it beat Pokémon FireRed start to finish using a vision-only harness, no maps, no game-state parsing, no navigation aids, just raw screen pixels.

Earlier Claude models needed a scaffold of helper tools to play at all. If your work is screenshot to code, scientific figure to numbers, or UI to spec, this is the jump that pays for the model.

- Memory compounds now.
Fable stays coherent across millions of tokens and improves its output from its own notes. Given file-based memory in a long game of Slay the Spire, it improved 3x more than Opus 4.8 did with the same memory, and reached the final act three times as often.

Persistent memory files were a minor optimization before. With Fable they're a force multiplier. Turn them on for any long-running task.

- It's token-efficient at lower effort.
On Cognition's FrontierCode benchmark, Fable leads frontier models even at medium effort. You do not have to crank it to maximum to beat Opus. Fable at medium already clears Opus 4.8 at its highest setting, for less money per task.

# The exact setup (copy-paste)

1. Set the model.
In Claude Code it's already live on Pro, Max, and Team. Switch in-session with /model, pick Claude Fable 5, and press d in the picker to make it your default. To persist it without touching the picker:

On the API the model id is `claude-fable-5`. At xhigh or max give it room to think and run subagents: start max_tokens around 64k and tune down.

2. Pick effort on purpose: it's a bigger lever than the model.
Fable runs five levels: low, medium, high, xhigh, max. Anthropic's own recommendation is xhigh for real coding and agent runs.
The cost curve says you often don't need to go higher: on FrontierCode's hardest tasks, Fable at medium already clears Opus 4.8 at its max, for a few dollars a task.

So the rule: xhigh for the long-horizon jobs you're spending the free window on (migrations, deep research, root-cause hunts), medium for everyday edits and questions, and treat max as mostly diminishing returns. In Claude Code, run `/effort xhigh` per session, or set effortLevel once in settings. Note max and ultracode are session-only. Set those with /effort, not in the settings file.

3. Turn persistent memory on for long tasks.
This is where the 3x lift lives. A long migration or a multi-session refactor with file-based memory is a different model than the same task without it.

4. Know when NOT to reach for Fable.
Two cases. First, short, cheap, deterministic prompts where Opus 4.8 or Sonnet returns the same answer for less money. The $10/$50 premium buys nothing there. Second, anything in the cyber, bio/chem, or distillation buckets. You'll get Opus 4.8 anyway, so call Opus directly and skip the fallback notice.

5. Mind the data retention.
Every Fable request is now retained for 30 days and analyzed in the background, specifically to catch jailbreak and distillation attempts and to reduce false positives. It is not used to train new models, and it's deleted after 30 days. But keep the retention in mind for sensitive client data, and don't try to jailbreak or extract anything: catching exactly that is what the retention exists for.

## Worth it vs overkill

Where the edge is real:

- Long-horizon coding. Codebase-wide migrations, multi-file refactors, anything where the task runs long enough for the lead to compound. This is the model's home turf.
- Vision. Screenshot to working code, dense scientific figures to extracted numbers, a UI to a spec. State-of-the-art, and a genuine jump over Opus.
- Senior-grade analysis. Finance, research, root-cause. One physics research partner reported Fable reached in 36 hours roughly where GPT-5.5 landed after four days, using a third of the reasoning tokens. Hebbia rated it the strongest finance model they've tested.

## Where it's overkill:

- Short, cheap, deterministic tasks. If Opus 4.8 or Sonnet gives the identical answer, the premium buys you nothing. Don't run a flagship to reformat JSON.
- Flagged-topic work. You're getting Opus 4.8 on those prompts regardless. Use Opus directly.

One trust note worth stating plainly, because the safety story scares people off: Fable and Mythos 5 are the same model, and on Anthropic's alignment assessment Mythos 5's misaligned behavior measured on par with Opus 4.8 and below earlier models. More capability did not come with a measurable alignment regression.
